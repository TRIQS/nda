29 /**
30 * @ingroup linalg_lapack
31 * @brief Interface to the LAPACK `%ggev` routine for real matrices.
32 *
33 * @details Computes the generalized eigenvalues \f$ \lambda_j = \alpha_j / \beta_j \f$ and, optionally, right/left
34 * eigenvectors \f$ \mathbf{v}_j \f$/\f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$ of a real generalized eigenvalue problem.
35 *
36 * The right eigenvector \f$ \mathbf{v}_j \f$ satisfies
37 * \f[
38 * \mathbf{A} \mathbf{v}_j = \lambda_j \mathbf{B} \mathbf{v}_j \; ,
39 * \f]
40 * whereas the left eigenvector \f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$ satisfies
41 * \f[
42 * \mathbf{u}_j^T \mathbf{A} = \lambda_j \mathbf{u}_j^T \mathbf{B} \; .
43 * \f]
44 * Here, \f$ \mathbf{u}_j^T \f$ denotes the transpose of \f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$.
45 *
46 * The eigenvalues are stored as real triples \f$ \alpha^{(r)}_j \f$, \f$ \alpha^{(i)}_j \f$ and \f$ \beta_j \f$ such
47 * that \f$ \alpha_j = \alpha^{(r)}_j + i \alpha^{(i)}_j \f$. If \f$ \alpha^{(i)}_j = 0 \f$ and \f$ \beta_j \neq 0
48 * \f$, then the eigenvalue is real.
49 *
50 * The quotients \f$ \alpha^{(r)}_j / \beta_j \f$ and \f$ \alpha^{(i)}_j / \beta_j \f$ may easily over- or underflow,
51 * and \f$ \beta_j \f$ may even be zero. Thus, the user should avoid naively computing the ratios. However, \f$
52 * \alpha^{(r)}_j \f$ and \f$ \alpha^{(i)}_j \f$ will be always less than and usually comparable with \f$ ||A|| \f$
53 * in magnitude, and \f$ \beta_j \f$ always less than and usually comparable with \f$ ||B|| \f$.
54 *
55 * The computed eigenvectors are scaled so that their largest components \f$ z \f$ satisfy \f$ |\mathrm{Re}(z)| +
56 * |\mathrm{Im}(z)| = 1 \f$.
57 *
58 * For real matrices, complex eigenvalues always occur in complex conjugate pairs and the corresponding eigenvectors
59 * are stored in a special packed format (see nda::linalg::unpack_eigenvectors).

62 * are required to have nda::F_layout.

144 /**
145 * @ingroup linalg_lapack
146 * @brief Interface to the LAPACK `%ggev` routine for complex matrices.
147 *
148 * @details Computes the generalized eigenvalues \f$ \lambda_j = \alpha_j / \beta_j \f$ and, optionally, right/left
149 * eigenvectors \f$ \mathbf{v}_j \f$/\f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$ of a complex generalized eigenvalue problem.
150 *
151 * The right eigenvector \f$ \mathbf{v}_j \f$ satisfies
152 * \f[
153 * \mathbf{A} \mathbf{v}_j = \lambda_j \mathbf{B} \mathbf{v}_j \; ,
154 * \f]
155 * whereas the left eigenvector \f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$ satisfies
156 * \f[
157 * \mathbf{u}_j^H \mathbf{A} = \lambda_j \mathbf{u}_j^H \mathbf{B} \; .
158 * \f]
159 * Here, \f$ \mathbf{u}_j^H \f$ denotes the conjugate-transpose of \f$ \mathbf{u}_j \f$.
160 *
161 * The eigenvalues are stored as complex pairs \f$ \alpha_j \f$ and \f$ \beta_j \f$. If \f$ \beta_j \neq 0 \f$, the
162 * eigenvalue is \f$ \lambda_j = \alpha_j / \beta_j \f$.
163 *
164 * The quotient \f$ \alpha_j / \beta_j \f$ may easily over- or underflow, and \f$ \beta_j \f$ may even be zero. Thus,
165 * the user should avoid naively computing the ratio. However, \f$ \alpha_j \f$ will be always less than and usually
166 * comparable with \f$ ||A|| \f$ in magnitude, and \f$ \beta_j \f$ always less than and usually comparable with \f$
167 * ||B|| \f$.
168 *
